# ONLINE-01 LLM summary evaluation — 2026-09-15

First recorded baseline for AI-generated summaries against a live model endpoint:
how often the shipped request path produces an answer the shipped validator
accepts, what it costs, and which defects the structural checks catch.

Raw records (generated by the runner, not hand-written):

| `online01-llm-eval-2026-09-15-run2.json` | before the fix: 3 requests per scope (9 requests, 13 calls) |
| `online01-llm-eval-2026-09-15-run3-after.json` | after the citation-contract fix: 3 requests per scope |
| `online01-llm-eval-2026-09-15-run4.json` | after the per-source ref fix: 6 requests per scope |

## How this was produced

`scripts/llm_summary_eval.py` (operator tooling). Per scope it builds the
provider input with the shipped `ai.build_summary_snapshot`, calls the shipped
retry policy `ai._generate_accepted`, classifies every attempt with the shipped
`ai._validate_llm_output`, and writes nothing to the database — no `ai_analyses`
row (no cache entry) and no `llm_usage` row. Verified around the run: both
tables stayed at 13 rows.

```bash
docker compose run --rm -v "$PWD/scripts:/app/scripts:ro" app \
  python /app/scripts/llm_summary_eval.py --yes --samples 3 --target-key TSLP --out - \
  > benchmarks/online01-llm-eval-<date>-run<N>.json
```

Provider billed every call, including the refused ones; the SPAgo ledger records
none of them.

| Scope | Requests | Answered | Provider calls | Answered on 1st attempt | Billed tokens | Median call latency |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| family | 6 | **6** | 7 | 5 | 31,541 | 6,280 ms |
| document | 6 | **5** | 7 | 5 | 14,953 | 5,215 ms |
| target | 6 | **5** | 9 | 3 | 95,154 | 7,336 ms |
| **pooled** | **18** | **16 (89%)** | **23** | **13 (72% of requests, 57% of calls)** | **141,648** | **6,179 ms** (max 9,690 ms) |

Two requests (1 document, 1 target) were refused twice and returned *no* summary;
the app shows its error state for those, and nothing invalid is persisted. The
target scope carries 67% of the token cost and produced the weakest result,
consistent with it being the only scope with a 30 KB input.

## Refusal classes the validator caught (7 refused calls)

| Class | Count | What the model did | Recovered by the re-sample |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Input key cited as a fact ref | 4 | cited `coverage`, `measurement_total`, `modality_breakdown`, `evidence_total`, `scaffolds`, `coverage:demo-fixture-v1`, … | 2 of 4 |
| Fabricated fact ref | 1 | cited `measurement:8531fefb-…`; **that id does not exist in the database** | yes |
| Malformed JSON | 2 | dropped a delimiter in the final array | 1 of 2 |

The dominant class is not random noise, and the re-sample does not correct it
(one document request produced the same class twice): every scope's input carries
aggregate blocks (`coverage`, `measurement_total`, `evidence_class_counts`,
`modality_breakdown`, `candidates_shown`, …) that have no `ref` field, while the
prompt required every paragraph to cite a ref from the input. A model that wants
to state a coverage or total fact had nothing to cite and used the key name.
Validation is right to refuse the fabricated identifier — a structurally valid
UUID that names no record is exactly the failure §12 exists to stop.

## What these numbers do and do not prove

- They prove: on this input, with this prompt and model, X% of calls are
  answerable within the shipped policy, which defects the validator catches, and
  what the run costs. They give a comparable number for a prompt or budget change.
- They do not prove capacity, other models' behaviour, or correctness of the
  *content*: schema validity is not scientific truth. One provider, one business
  day, one fixture family/document and one live target; 18 requests is small
  enough that a single call moves the rate by ~5 points.
- Not covered: other targets (a sparse target with failed sources is not
  measured), non-demo families, budget exhaustion, cancellation, and the app's
  presentation of the refused-request error state (checked earlier in
  `docs/plans/2026-09-15-llm-live-smoke.md` §10).

## After the citation-contract fix (run 3)

What changed: `_COMMON_RULES` rule 3 now states that only the input's `ref`
fields are fact refs (an input key such as `coverage` is not one), and that a
coverage or totals statement is cited with the input's root ref; the prompt
versions moved to `family-summary-v6`, `document-summary-v5`,
`target-investigation-v5`. The snapshot JSON is byte-identical to the before
runs, so the only difference the model sees is the instruction.

| Scope | Requests | Answered before → after | First-attempt before → after | Billed tokens before → after |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| family | 6 → 3 | 6/6 → **3/3** | 5/6 → **3/3** | 31,541 → 13,138 |
| document | 6 → 3 | 5/6 → **3/3** | 5/6 → **3/3** | 14,953 → 9,508 |
| target | 6 → 3 | 5/6 → **1/3** | 3/6 → **1/3** | 95,154 → 68,178 |
| pooled | 18 → 9 | 16 (89%) → **7 (78%)** | 13 (72%) → 7 (78%) | 141,648 → 90,824 |

Refusal classes: before `key-cited ×4, not-an-input-ref ×1, json_invalid ×2`;
after `not-an-input-ref ×2, key-cited ×1, json_invalid ×1`.

What moved, and what did not:

- **Family and document are clean**: 6 of 6 calls accepted on the first attempt
  after the change, with no citation refusal at all (before: 10 of 12 calls
  accepted first-try, both refusals of the key-citation class). The root-ref
  instruction is followed where the root is the natural owner of the aggregate
  facts, and both scopes pass with no re-sample.
- **The target scope did not improve**, and its failures are not the same class
  wearing the same name: the model still has nothing legal to cite for the
  per-source coverage blocks, so it now cites the *dataset-version strings* those
  blocks contain (`uniprot:2026-09-15`, `bindingdb:2026-09-15`) or their keys
  (`coverage`, `measurement_total`). One call in three was accepted; two requests
  spent two calls each and returned no summary at all — 54,494 tokens producing
  nothing, over half of the after-run's spend.
- **Total request success did not rise** (89% → 78%, 9 requests) and is not
  claimed to: with n=9 a single call moves it by ~11 points, and the target scope
  was unlucky twice. The defensible statement is per-scope: the instruction
  change removed the class in the two scopes whose aggregate facts have a natural
  root ref, and it did not remove it in the one scope that exposes per-source
  blocks.

**Conclusion for the next round:** instruction wording has reached its limit. The
target snapshot exposes facts (`sources[*]` status, kept counts, `dataset_version`,
`coverage`) that cannot be cited at all, so the model must either omit them — 
contradicting "state the coverage" — or invent a ref. The fix is structural: give
each source block its own ref, add it to `allowed_refs` **and** to
`_build_citations` (`by_ref`) with a `source` citation kind, and extend rule 3.
Note the UI consequence, which is why this is its own round: `_build_citations`
currently *drops* a ref it has no metadata for, so adding refs to the allowed set
without the citation kind would silently show a paragraph with no visible
support. Acceptance for that round: run 4 with the same runner must reduce
`not-an-input-ref`/`key-cited` to zero in the target scope, with a browser check
of the new citation chip.

## After the structural per-source ref fix (run 4)

What changed: the target snapshot's `sources[*]` and `coverage[*]` rows now carry
`ref: source:<name>` (the coverage row also carries `source_name`), `allowed_refs`
collects them, `_build_citations` maps them to a `source` citation kind whose label
states the retrieval (`chembl · complete · 111 kept of 114 seen ·
chembl:2026-09-15`), and the target system prompt states the per-source ref rule.
`TARGET_PROMPT_VERSION` → `target-investigation-v6`; the family and document
prompts and their cached analyses are unchanged (`family-summary-v6`,
`document-summary-v5`).

Input change, measured: target snapshot 30,231 → **30,411 bytes** (bound 32,768),
citable refs 52 → **55**, and `measurements_listed` / `candidates_shown` unchanged
(50 of 114 measurements, 1 candidate) — the added refs did not push the snapshot
into extra trimming. Family (6,792 B / 19 refs) and document (4,014 B / 10 refs)
are byte-identical to the earlier runs.

Run 4 was taken at `--samples 6` per scope rather than 3, because the claim under
test is a *rate* in the target scope and n=3 cannot separate "fixed" from "less
likely" (run 3's target result was 1 clean first attempt in 3 requests).

| Scope | Requests | Answered before → after | First-attempt before → after | Calls | Billed tokens before → after |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| family | 6 → 6 | 6/6 → **6/6** | 5/6 → **6/6** | 6 | 31,541 → 26,868 |
| document | 6 → 6 | 5/6 → **6/6** | 5/6 → **5/6** | 7 | 14,953 → 21,453 |
| target | 6 → 6 | 5/6 → **6/6** | 3/6 → **6/6** | 6 | 95,154 → 84,253 |
| pooled | 18 → 18 | 16 (89%) → **18 (100%)** | 13 (72%) → **17 (94%)** | 19 | 141,648 → 132,574 |

(The "before" column pools runs 1 + 2, the same six-request-per-scope baseline the
earlier table uses; the "after" column is run 4 alone. Median call latency
6,156 ms, max 13,922 ms.)

Refusal classes: before `key-cited ×4, not-an-input-ref ×1, json_invalid ×2`;
after run 3 `not-an-input-ref ×2, key-cited ×1, json_invalid ×1`; after run 4
**`ref without its namespace prefix ×1`**.

What this run shows, with the evidence for the claim rather than the rate alone:

- **Every one of the six target requests answered on the first attempt, and every
  one cited all three per-source refs.** The accepted JSON in the artifact was
  checked directly: each run's paragraphs include a per-source paragraph citing
  `source:bindingdb`, `source:chembl`, `source:pubchem` (3 source refs per run,
  alongside `target:`/`candidate:`/`measurement:` refs). So the fix is *used*, not
  merely tolerated — the refusal is gone because the fact now has a legal citation,
  and the summary states the per-source status the prompt asked for.
- **The target scope's refusals are gone**: 4 refused calls in 9 calls across runs
  1–2, 4 in 5 in run 3, **0 in 6 in run 4**.
- **The one remaining refusal is a different, better-understood class**: a document
  request cited `facce3fd-60c9-…` instead of `document:facce3fd-60c9-…` (and two
  other bare UUIDs). The validator is right to refuse it — a bare UUID does not say
  whether it names a document, an evidence record or a candidate — and the bounded
  re-sample recovered that request, so it answered at 6/6 after two calls.
- **Pooled request success reached 18/18 for the first time**, on twice the sample
  count of run 3. With n=6 per scope a single call still moves a per-scope rate by
  ~17 points, so the defensible reading is the counts: 17 of 19 calls accepted
  first-attempt and the only refusal belonged to a scope that was already clean.

Not proven by this run: that the class cannot recur (one provider, one day, one
live target); that the summary *content* is scientifically correct (schema and
citation validity are not truth); that other endpoints behave the same; and that a
sparse or failed-source target behaves like this one — TSLP's three sources all
returned `complete`, whereas the demo target (`DEMO-TARGET-1`) has no retrievals at
all and was not exercised in this run.

Browser consequence, verified in the running app (1510×927, current checkout built
into the image, `spago-app-1` healthy): a live LLM target summary in the app
produced 18 stored citations of which **3 are `source`** (`ai_analyses` row
`f1df060f-…`, `target-investigation-v6`, `llm_inferred`), the panel renders them as
`SOURCE bindingdb · complete · 3 kept · bindingdb:2026-09-15`, and clicking the
chembl chip applies `coverage-chip-focused` to the header's
`#coverage-source-chembl` chip for 2.4 s (computed `outline: solid 2px`, accent
background) before clearing. A `measurement` citation in the same panel still
switches to the Evidence tab, so the routing change did not take over the existing
behaviour.
